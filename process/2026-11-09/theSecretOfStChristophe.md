# The secret of St Christophe
Claire CHAFFARD

## Project
A story of idolatry, jealousy and bullying among girls at a Catholic school, where a cult devoted to their male teacher exposes their desperate research for acceptance and belonging. 
## Pitch
What happens when an all-girls Catholic school hires its first male teacher? Jealousy, bullying, and idolatry. Within the cult that they form to worship their male teacher, they criticise each other in-person and online for not performing the cult rituals, for doing them too often, too extremely, or simply not in the “right” way. Each girl is trying to find how exactly they can behave to get what they think they need, while blind to the thing that could actually free them: faith in each other.
## Setup
At Saint Christophe all girls Catholic school, the students have never had a male teacher. But when the school bell rang for the first period general science class, in walked Pierre Lafleur -- a young, put-together new teacher with new beliefs.
## Personas

Cf: [persona cards](PersonaCards.pdf) & persona map 

In this story, we can find six main characters, each driven by a different need of acceptance, recognition and belonging. 

At the center is Pierre Lafleur, the school's first male science teacher, a former Catholic who strongly believes in science as the ultimate truth. He wants to inspire a new generation of curious mind, he remains completely unaware of the cult forming around him, mistaking the girl’s devotion as religious piety. 

Cécile, the cult leader is a charismatic and admired student who creates the cult as a way to build the sense of family and belonging she lacks at home. As being empathetic, she maintains her authority through control, social pressure and an obsession with appearances, while struggling to hide her true identity. 

Her second-in-command, Aurélie, The Bookkeeper, is academically gifted but constantly overlooked in favour of Cécile’s popularity and beauty. She becomes increasingly strategic and seeks Cécile’s position. 

Daphé, The True believer, is the most devoted and obsessive member of the group. Insecure and desperate for validation, she idealises Pierre as saviour and takes the cult’s rituals to extremes, eventually putting the group ‘s secrecy at risk. 

In contrast, Therese, the Scapegoat, is deeply religious and sees Pierre’s scientific worldview as an opportunity to question and strengthen her own Catholic faith. Her sincere attempts to share her beliefs and challenge the others’ perspectives gradually make her an outsider and a target of the group. 

Eloise, the follower, is a hardworking but academically average student who is constantly compared to her brothers by her parents. She sees Pierre’s science lessons and extra tutoring as a path towards a scholarship in mechanical engineering and, ultimately, the parental recognition she longs for. She doesn’t genuinely believe in the cult, she participates in its rituals to avoid exclusion and protect her relationship with Pierre. 

Together, these characters create a complex web of admiration, jealousy, rivalry and dependence, each seeking something different from the same collective, while remaining blind to the solidarity that could truly fulfil their need for belonging.
